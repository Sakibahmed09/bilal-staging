Setting it up

Do I need an account?

No. No sign-up, no email, no tracking. Pick your mosque once and the screen remembers.

Does my phone need to be on the same wifi as the screen?

No. The code travels over the internet, so any phone works from anywhere. It does not need to be the same Amazon account either.

How long does setup take?

Three moments. Open the site on the screen, scan the code with your phone, then press once for sound. The screen picks up the rest on its own.

I paired it to the wrong mosque. How do I change it?

Add ?setup=1 to the address on the screen and the pairing screen comes back. Pair it again and the new mosque replaces the old one.

Does the pairing survive a reboot?

Yes, and a power cut. It is stored on the screen itself, so it comes back paired without your phone.

Uninstalling the app is the one thing that clears it, because the storage goes with the app.

The athan

It says the athan is blocked by the browser.

Browsers keep sound locked until somebody presses a button, so the screen cannot play anything until you press once. Press OK on the remote, or tap the screen, and the footer changes to athan ready.

The Fire TV app presses it for you, which is most of the reason it exists.

It played for a few days and then went silent.

Look at the footer first. If it does not say athan ready, the screen reloaded at some point and is waiting for a press. One press re-arms it.

If the footer says it is ready and the athan still did not play, that is a real fault and worth reporting.

Can I run it silent?

Add ?quiet=1 to the address. It shows the times and never makes a sound.

Why is maghrib different?

Maghrib jama'ah is usually only minutes after the adhan, so a fifteen minute warning would land before the adhan itself. Maghrib gets leave now to catch jama'ah. Every other prayer gets the athan at the start time.

The screen

It was dark when I came down for Fajr.

Three different timers can do this, and they are not the same thing. Which one you have depends on what the screen did:

  • The television powered off completely. Nearly every TV switches off after about four hours with no button pressed. The setting is on the TV, under Eco, Energy Saving, or no-operation off timer.
  • The TV is on but says no signal. That is Fire OS asking Still Watching? after four hours with no press on the remote, then sleeping the stick. Turn it off under Settings, Preferences, Data Usage Monitoring, Still Watching. If the question is on screen, Don't show again does the same thing in one press.
  • It went dark within the hour. Fire TV has a second sleep timer, around fourteen minutes, that Amazon do not expose in any settings screen. The app retires it. The browser route cannot.
The screen flickers.

Add ?lite=1 to the address. That drops the drifting layers and the moving sky, which is what a weaker GPU struggles with.

If the flicker stops, running it that way permanently is fine. You lose some atmosphere and nothing else.

It was offline all night. Did it show me the wrong times?

No. With no connection it shows the timetable it already has and says so in the footer, with how old it is. It does not invent times, and it does not go blank.

Do I need to update it?

No. The screen refreshes itself overnight, and the app is a window onto the website, so a fix reaches every screen without anybody reinstalling anything.

The times

Where do the times come from?

From what your mosque publishes itself. Bilal does not calculate jama'ah times, because a guessed time makes you late.

My mosque is not on the list.

You can add it yourself from your phone. Photograph or upload your mosque's timetable and it reads the year off it, showing you anything it is unsure about so you can correct it before it goes live.

It then exists for everybody else at your mosque, not only you.

The times are wrong.

Report it from the link at the bottom of this page. It asks which device and what you saw, which is what makes it fixable.

If the screen is showing a code in the corner, include it. It links your note to what that screen was actually doing.

Why does it say the times are estimated?

Some mosques publish the adhan times and no jama'ah times. When that happens the screen says so rather than presenting an estimate as your mosque's own decision.

What happens when the timetable runs out?

It tells you three weeks before, then stops showing jama'ah times rather than guessing into a new year. You add the new timetable the same way you set it up the first time.

Devices

What can I run it on?

A Fire TV stick, an Android or Google TV, an Echo Show, a spare phone or tablet, or a tab on a laptop. Anything with a browser and a screen you can leave on.

Which is best?

A screen that stays put. The habit comes from a fixed display in a room you walk through, on all day, that you never have to open.

A laptop tab is for hearing it work in the next few minutes, or for a desk you sit at all day.

The app will not install on my Fire Stick.

Amazon ended sideloading on their newest sticks, which is the Fire TV Stick 4K Select from 2025 and the Fire TV Stick HD from 2026 onwards. Those run the browser route instead, which works, with the fourteen minute sleep timer as the catch.

Every Fire OS 7 and 8 device installs it normally.

What does the app ask for?

The internet, and nothing else. No account, no tracking, no permissions beyond that.

Can I run it on a laptop?

Open the site, press F for fullscreen, and leave the tab open.

Stop the computer sleeping if you want Fajr. A sleeping screen calls nothing, and it will not tell you it missed one.
